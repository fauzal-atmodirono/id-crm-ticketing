### 1.2 The gaps that matter

Ranked by how many requirements they block and how hard they are to close.

| # | Gap | Blocks | Size | Depends on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **No telephony instrumentation, and no call queue at all.** Abandon rate, AQT, AHT, % answered in 20 s, After-Call-Work — none exist. The reporting UI says so in its own words (`ProtonCallCentrePlaceholder.vue`). Worse, there is no `<Enqueue>` / TaskRouter anywhere: calls `<Dial>` a number and fall to voicemail after 20 s, so **abandon rate has no queue to measure**. | 3.1.2, 4.63, 4.64, 4.66, 4.69, B-IVR-08, B-IVR-09, C1-02, C1-03, C1-11 (call half), and **6 of the 14 monthly control items** | **4–6 weeks** — build a real queue (Twilio TaskRouter or `<Enqueue>`), ingest call events, then the metrics | a decision on Twilio Studio vs the AI bridge (below); Package C being run against a real call at all |
| **2** | **SLA enforcement runs on a wall clock; every client target is in working hours.** `sla.py:129` computes age as `(now - created_at)`, straight through nights, weekends and public holidays. The *reporting* half is working-hours-aware (`metrics/business_hours.py`), so alerts and reports will disagree with each other. Compounding it, the in-hours/out-of-hours flag is computed transiently in `lifecycle_scanner.py:143` and **never persisted**, so no report can split by it. | 3.1.3, 3.2.4, 4.34, 4.52, 4.53, 4.54, B-WA-14, B-SM-06, B-EM-05, C1-12 items 7/8/13/14 | **1–2 weeks** — reuse `working_minutes_between` in the enforcement path; persist a `received_in_business_hours` attribute at intake | nothing; the working-hours helper already exists |
| **3** | **Escalation to a dealer/PIC only fires on Email conversations.** Appendix B makes "Escalate email to Dealer/PIC" the terminal step of the **WhatsApp, Social and Email** flows. Adding the `escalate` label to a WhatsApp case notifies nobody. WhatsApp is **73% of weekly volume** (C2 p1). | B-WA-17, B-SM-09, 4.29–4.34 in practice | **1–2 weeks** — the EM-7 flow is channel-gated, not channel-coupled; lift the gate and source the customer address from the contact record | a decision on what "acknowledge the customer" means when the customer is on WhatsApp, not email |

Three more that are strategically larger than they are technically hard:

- **§7 License Control has no counterpart at all.** 4 of 7 requirements are
  outright GAP (cryptographic key binding, online/offline validation, session
  heartbeat & concurrency polling, per-user license provisioning). The platform
  is self-hosted open-source Chatwoot with no licensing subsystem, because it
  was never meant to be a licensed product. **This is a commercial-model
  question before it is an engineering one** — PRO-NET appears to be
  specifying a vendor-licensed product, and the proposed answer is a
  self-managed deployment. Raise it before it is answered feature-by-feature.
- **99.9% uptime (8.1.5) and P1 `<2h` resolution 24/7 (§9) against a single
  GCE VM running Docker Compose.** There is no HA, no failover, no second
  zone; `deploy/scripts/backup.sh` keeps 7 days of `pg_dump` **on the same
  VM**, and there is no restore script and no monitoring/alerting stack in
  `deploy/` at all. 99.9% is 43 minutes a month. The architecture cannot
  support what §8/§9 commit to, and 8.1.6 additionally requires RSA to stay up
  *through* scheduled maintenance on that same host.
- **DMS/TSP is a mock, and eight requirements sit on top of it.**
  `dms_client.py` ships `NullDmsClient` (what every tenant runs) and
  `MockDmsClient` (every field stamped `"(Demo data)"`). There is no TSP client
  at all — "TSP" is a label on the DMS card. Chassis/VIN does not exist
  anywhere in the data model, and C2's WIP table (p13) prints it per case.

`docs/proposals/PRO-NET RFP 2026_028 - Devoteam Vendor Response (Technical).docx`

## Addendum 2026-08-09 — P3 delivered, and what it deliberately does not close

Package P3 (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-08-08-rfp-p3-case-record-extensions.md`)
is built. Ten case columns now reach the warehouse, `case_detail` joins the
concern pivot, and `case_state` becomes its own trend series beside — never
instead of — Chatwoot's `status`.

**C1-07's "escalated to HQ" column will report zero, and that is correct.**
`escalated_to` ships with `dealer` and `none` only. Nothing in the case model
distinguishes an HQ escalation from any other, which is **client question Q5**,
still unanswered. The validator rejects `hq` by name and says why, and the
provisioning script offers no `hq` option, so the value cannot enter the
warehouse by accident. A plausible wrong number on a headline slide is worse
than a truthful zero — but the zero must be captioned as "not yet classified",
not read as "no HQ escalations happened".

**C2-09 and the 297-vs-264 discrepancy (Q8).** `v_concern_pivot` buckets a
null `case_detail` as `Unspecified` rather than filtering it out, so the
pivot's total always reconciles with the headline case count. This does not
fix the existing C2 discrepancy — it avoids creating a second instance of it.

**Coverage.** Most P3 columns are typed by an agent under time pressure.
`REPORT_COVERAGE_DISCLOSURE` (default ON) attaches a coverage note to any block
grouped by one, because a chart of three plate numbers is a chart of the three
agents who filled the field in, not of the fleet.
