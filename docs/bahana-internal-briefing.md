# Bahana — What We Have

**Internal briefing for the business team.** Read before talking to the client.
Ten minutes.

Not for sharing outside Devoteam. The customer-facing version is a separate
document.

---

## In one paragraph

We built a working AI assistant for Bahana Sekuritas that talks to investors on
WhatsApp. It recognises who is messaging, reads their portfolio, answers their
question, and introduces a product that suits them — and refuses to suggest one
that does not. A human can take over the conversation at any moment. It runs on
fictional customer data we generated ourselves, because Bahana has given us none.

---

## The three claims we can defend

Everything else is detail. These are what the demo actually proves.

**1. It knows the customer.**
Ask "what shares do I hold?" and it names them — from the customer record, not
invented. Ask the same question as a different investor and the answer changes.

**2. It will not mis-sell.**
A conservative investor asking for "the highest return" is not offered an equity
product. The rule about who may be offered what is stored **as data** and applied
*before* the AI is involved. It is not the model being careful, and it cannot be
talked out of it. This is the strongest thing we have for a regulated buyer.

**3. A person stays in control.**
Every conversation lives in a CRM where a human reads the same customer profile
the AI sees, and can take over mid-conversation — at which point the AI goes
silent. Every AI decision is logged with its model and cost.

## The data we hold

All synthetic, all generated by us, all in a proper warehouse rather than a
spreadsheet. Structure matters here — it is what lets us answer "how do you stop
it recommending the wrong thing" with a query instead of a paragraph.

| What | Count | Why it matters commercially |
|---|---|---|
| Customer master | 25 nasabah | Risk profile, AUM band, cash balance, months since last trade — the fields personalization actually needs |
| Product catalogue | 7 products | Money market, bonds, mixed funds, equity funds, IPO, direct shares — each with a risk level |
| **Eligibility rules** | 6 rules | **Which products may be offered to which risk profile.** The compliance story, as a table |
| Instruments | 10 IDX tickers | Real listed companies with sector, so holdings look plausible |
| Holdings | per customer | What each investor actually owns |
| Next-best offer | per customer | The single product selected for each customer, with the reason |

### Two numbers worth quoting in a meeting

**Zero.** Offers that fall outside the eligibility rules. Provable live in front
of them.

**Four.** Conservative investors in our data who hold shares directly — riskier
than their stated profile. That is a genuine advisory finding, and it is the
natural opening to the conversation about their real data: *"we found this in 25
fictional customers; what would we find in your book?"*

Eight scenarios across three investor profiles, run end to end.

| Behaviour | Result |

## What is **not** built

Do not imply otherwise. Every one of these is a normal next phase, not a gap we
are hiding.

**No identity verification.** The demo shows balances to whoever holds the phone.
In production, figures would be released only after the customer is verified. We
raise this ourselves rather than waiting to be asked — it reads as competence.

**No connection to Bahana systems.** Not one field comes from them.

**No relationship-manager view.** The daily list of clients worth contacting,
each with a drafted message, is designed and specified but not built. This is
probably the thing they will most want, so be careful how you describe it.

**No mass messaging.** WhatsApp does not permit free outbound messaging, and we
are not proposing to work around it. See below.

---

## The two commercial points that matter most

### 1. Personalization quality is capped by their data

Qontak holds conversation contacts — name, phone, maybe tags. Genuine
personalization for a securities firm needs risk profile, holdings, transaction
recency and product gaps, which live in their back office.

**Whether Bahana gives us a data feed decides whether this is a next-best-action
product or segment-by-tag messaging with better copy.** Scope and price
accordingly. Present it as two levels of outcome, not as a precondition — they
can start without it, and the value of adding it becomes obvious.

### 2. We are not proposing to blast

They asked for "suggestion or blast". WhatsApp only permits free-form messaging
inside a 24-hour window that opens when the *customer* messages first. Outside
it, every message is a paid, pre-approved template.

Our answer is deliberate, and it is a better product: personalize inside
conversations the customer starts, and stage the offer so it is delivered the
next time they get in touch for any reason. No templates, no marketing fees, and
far easier to defend to a regulator than pushing offers at retail investors.

**The consequence to be honest about:** because we never message first, something
has to bring customers into WhatsApp — links in their app, QR codes on
statements, click-to-WhatsApp ads. That is a joint workstream, not our problem
alone, and it should be in the proposal rather than discovered later.

## What not to say

- **No prices, rates, or timelines** that have not been agreed internally.
- **No WhatsApp messaging cost figures** without checking the current rate card.
  Meta changed its pricing repeatedly through 2025.
- **No regulation numbers** unless verified. Talk about the obligation —
  suitability, licensed recommendations, consent — not the article number.
- **Never disparage Qontak**, and never speculate about their contract. We are
  proposing a layer they do not have, not a replacement.
- **The demo proves the mechanism, not model quality on their book.** Nobody can
  prove that without their data. Saying so first protects everything else we say.

---

## Questions worth asking them

1. Can you give us a feed from the back office — risk profile, holdings,
   transactions? *(This decides the shape of everything.)*
2. Does customer data have to stay in your environment? *(Changes the deployment
   model entirely.)*
3. Who owns the product catalogue, and who signs off on suitability rules?
4. Are you keeping Qontak? *(Affects whose WhatsApp number we use.)*
5. Where could you put links that bring customers into WhatsApp?
