**Date:** 2026-08-22
**Status:** design, approved for Phase 0 build
**Branch:** dev-yuda
**Demo date:** Monday 2026-08-24
**Audience:** two — the engineer building Phase 0, and the agent writing the

---

## 1. What Bahana asked for

Bahana Sekuritas wants **personalisasi dan peningkatan interaksi nasabah**.
They put two options on the table:

- **Opsi 1** — they already hold contact/profile data in **Mekari Qontak** but
  don't use it. They want personalization: targeted offers, with the output
  shaped as a **suggestion** to their people or a **blast** straight to the
  customer.
- **Opsi 2** — build the CRM and its AI from scratch, possibly reusing Proton
  assets.

Their own stated priority is the **AI personalization**, not the CRM. Qontak is
an incumbent SaaS and they are candid that a CRM proposal probably shouldn't
try to displace it.

### 1.1 We propose a third framing

Don't compete with Qontak as a SaaS CRM, and don't build a thin AI layer on top
of Qontak either. The first is a fight we'd win slowly if at all; the second
leaves us dependent on somebody else's API with nowhere to put the workflow
that makes the AI useful.

Propose an **AI personalization and engagement layer that owns its own
workspace** — our CRM — which reads their customer data, produces personalized
conversations and relationship-manager suggestions, and delivers over WhatsApp.
Qontak can stay in place for service conversations. If the layer proves out, the
CRM absorbs the rest naturally.

That is a smaller first sale and a considerably larger second one.

## 2. The product idea: personalization without templates

### 2.1 The constraint everything bends around

WhatsApp does not let you message a customer freely. Outside a **24-hour
customer service window** — opened only when the customer messages you — every
outbound message must be a pre-approved **template**, priced per message by
Meta with a Twilio fee on top, and marketing templates require opt-in.

**Decision: we use no templates, in the demo or in production.** This is a
deliberate product choice, not only a cost one. It removes Meta template review
from the delivery path, removes marketing-category messaging fees, and — most
importantly — it forces the design toward conversations the customer started,
which is both cheaper and far easier to defend to a regulator than push
advertising to investors.

> **Verify before quoting numbers.** Meta's WhatsApp pricing moved from
> conversation-based to per-message during 2025, with category-dependent rates
> and free allowances for some utility traffic inside an open window. The shape
> of the argument above is stable; specific rates are not. Check the current
> Meta and Twilio rate cards before any figure enters a commercial document.

### 2.2 So how do we get "blast" outcomes?

Their brief asks for suggestion **or** blast. Blast is off the table. The
answer that recovers most of its value at zero messaging cost:

**Stage the offer; fire it on the next inbound message.**

A batch job computes a next-best-offer per nasabah and parks it on their
profile. When that nasabah next messages — for any reason at all, a fee
question, a password problem, an account query — the AI answers the actual
question first and then weaves the staged offer in, in context, as free-form
text inside the open window.

This is campaign semantics with conversational delivery:

- no template, no approval queue, no marketing-category fee;
- no 24-hour-window problem, because the customer opened the window;
- the offer arrives attached to a real conversation instead of interrupting
  the customer's day, which is why it will convert better than a blast;
- and it demos well: once the RM surface exists (Phase 2) you can show the
  queue of staged offers and then a live conversation consuming one. Phase 0
  shows only the conversation half.

### 2.3 What still has to drive inbound

Because we never message first, **something must bring nasabah into WhatsApp.**
This is the real bottleneck in production and it must be an explicit part of
the proposal, not an afterthought:

- click-to-WhatsApp entry points in their app and website;
- QR codes on e-statements and monthly portfolio reports;
- a WhatsApp CTA in the emails they already send;
- click-to-WhatsApp ads, which additionally open a free entry-point window.

Email and in-app remain available as genuine bulk channels for anything that
truly needs to go out unprompted. Use WhatsApp for the **conversation**, other
channels for the **reach**.

## 3. Constraints that shape the design

### 3.1 There is no customer data yet

Bahana has provided none. Phase 0 therefore runs entirely on **synthetic
data**, generated by us, clearly labelled as such on screen.

This is worth stating plainly in the proposal, because it bounds what the demo
proves. It demonstrates the **mechanism** — that the AI can hold a
portfolio-aware conversation and that a human can take over mid-flow. It does
not demonstrate model quality on their actual book, which nobody can
demonstrate without their data.

The corollary is the single most important commercial point in this document:
**personalization quality is capped by the data feed.** Qontak holds
conversation contacts — name, phone, maybe tags. Real personalization for a
securities firm needs AUM, risk profile, holdings, transaction recency and
product gaps, which live in the back office, not in Qontak. Whether Bahana will
provide that feed decides whether this is a genuine next-best-action product or
segment-by-tag messaging with better copy. Scope and price accordingly.

### 3.2 Regulatory

- **Investment recommendations are a licensed activity.** An AI that
  autonomously tells a nasabah to buy a specific security is a regulatory
  problem. The product must present **offers, education and conversation
  starters**, with securities recommendations routed through a licensed human.
  Our `AGENT_MODE=suggest` pattern already has exactly this shape and should be
  sold as a compliance feature, not a limitation.
- **UU PDP No. 27/2022.** Using personal data to target an offer is marketing
  use and requires consent, plus a working opt-out — even when the customer
  started the conversation.
- **Suitability.** Offer eligibility must respect the nasabah's risk profile.
  See §7.4 — this belongs in code, not in a prompt.

### 3.3 Reuse

The conversational half of this product already exists and runs in production
for other tenants: WhatsApp via Twilio into Chatwoot, an agent-bot AI loop with
human interruption, operator-editable persona, a vector knowledge base, RBAC,
per-tenant isolation, and a deterministic demo-data seeder. The genuinely new
subsystem is personalization: profiles, segments, offers, scoring and staging.

## 4. Architecture

### 4.1 Conversation path — exists today

```
 Nasabah ──WhatsApp──► Twilio ──► Chatwoot (CRM)
                                    │  owns the channel, contact,
                                    │  conversation and full history
                                    │  human agent can interrupt at any point
                                    │
                                    ▼  POST /webhooks/chatwoot/bot  (HMAC)
                              agent/ orchestrator
                                    │  debounce, fetch history,
                                    │  build prompt, call Gemini,
                                    │  log to ai_actions
                                    ▼
                              reply back through Chatwoot ──► Twilio ──► WhatsApp
```

Human interruption is not a feature we add; it is how the loop already works.
The orchestrator acts only on conversations in `pending` status, so the moment
an agent replies the conversation leaves `pending` and the bot falls silent.

We deliberately do **not** use the AEON360 external-brain topology here. That
pattern exists because AEON360 owned a brain we could not move, so Chatwoot
became pure transport across an HTTP seam. For Bahana we own both sides, so the
native agent-bot path is one hop shorter and is what already runs in
production. Keep the external-brain seam in reserve for one scenario only: if
Bahana requires that nasabah data never leave their infrastructure, the
personalization service moves into their project and we fall back to that
shape.

### 4.2 Personalization data — three tiers, deliberately separate

Conflating these is the most likely way to build something that demos and then
fails in production.

| Tier | Store | Job | Latency budget |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Analytical** | BigQuery | Nightly batch: segments, eligibility, scoring. Landing zone for the back-office feed. | minutes |
| **Serving** | Backend per-tenant Postgres | Materialized `nasabah_profile` keyed by phone + current staged offer. Read during a chat turn. | milliseconds |
| **Display** | Chatwoot contact custom attributes | Curated subset rendered in the agent sidebar; drives Chatwoot's native contact filters. | n/a |

BigQuery must **never** sit in the chat hot path — it is seconds and costs per
query. We already run a real BigQuery layer for metrics (view-per-dataclass,
`ensure_views()`), so the analytical tier is credible in a proposal without
being speculative.

**Phase 0 skips BigQuery entirely.** With synthetic data there is nothing to
warehouse; the seeder writes straight to the display tier. BigQuery appears on
the architecture slide and arrives when a real feed does.

### 4.3 The AI does three separable jobs

Keeping them separate is what makes the system auditable, and auditability is
what a compliance reviewer will ask for.

1. **Who** — eligibility and scoring. Deterministic rules first; a model later,
   only if the data justifies it. Never an LLM.
2. **What** — offer selection from a catalog the compliance team owns. Never an
   LLM.
3. **How** — drafting the message: tone, framing, weaving the offer into
   whatever the customer actually asked about. This is the LLM's only job, and
   it may only phrase offers it was handed.

If the LLM can invent the offer, no rule you write about suitability is
enforceable.

### 4.4 Staged offers

`nasabah_profile` carries a `next_best_offer` (id, rationale, valid-until).
The batch writes it; the orchestrator reads it and passes it into the prompt as
context with an instruction to introduce it only when it fits the conversation
naturally, and never at the expense of answering the customer's question.
Delivery is recorded so the same offer is not repeated, and `ai_actions`
retains the decision with its inputs.

### 4.5 The CRM's own automation engine

Chatwoot ships a rule engine (events → conditions → actions) that this platform
had never switched on for any tenant. It composes with everything above without
new plumbing, because it reads the same contact custom attributes the
orchestrator reads and writes labels our webhook already consumes. That is what
makes the "who" half of §4.3 operator-editable rather than ours to deploy.

Designed and provisioned separately — see
**`docs/bahana-automation-personalization.md`** and
`deploy/scripts/provision-bahana-automation.py`.

## 5. Phase 0 — the Monday demo

**Goal:** show that the AI recognizes a nasabah, personalizes using their
portfolio, and that a human can take the conversation over at any moment —
live, on WhatsApp, in a CRM that looks like Bahana's.

### 5.1 Scope decision: no fork patches

The one deploy path in this repo with real failure modes is the Chatwoot custom
image: Cloud Build off-VM for `amd64`, push to Artifact Registry, then pull and
`--force-recreate` on the VM. A local `arm64` build silently fails the VM's
pull. With two days, that path is not worth entering.

Patch `0041`'s Customer 360 page is hand-written Vue that knows nothing about
generic blocks, so a portfolio panel there is a new patch and therefore a
Chatwoot image rebuild. **Cut it.**

We do not need it. **Chatwoot renders contact custom attributes natively in the
conversation sidebar** with no patch at all — attributes are defined once in the
admin UI. The agent sees risk profile, AUM band, holdings and staged offer
directly beside the conversation they are about to interrupt, which is a better
demo beat than a separate lookup page.

Phase 0 therefore touches **`agent/` and the seeder only**. Both are light and
built on the VM. No Cloud Build, no Chatwoot image rebuild.

### 5.2 What we build

1. **Sekuritas preset in the demo-data seeder**
   (`deploy/scripts/seed_demo_data/`). A vocabulary and profile module beside
   the automotive one, generating synthetic nasabah: name, phone, risk profile
   (konservatif / moderat / agresif), AUM band, RDN balance, holdings across
   real IDX tickers, last transaction date, dormancy, product gaps, and a
   staged next-best-offer.

   This is data shapes, not machinery. The generator is already deterministic
   (`random.Random(seed)` threaded throughout, byte-identical for a given
   `(count, batch_id, seed)`), batch-scoped so runs don't collide on Chatwoot's
   phone/email uniqueness, and purge-safe. `create_contact` in `client.py`
   already writes contact `custom_attributes`, including the create-then-PATCH
   fallback for Chatwoot's create endpoint not reliably persisting them.

2. **Customer context in the orchestrator prompt** (`agent/app/services/`).
   `_build_system_prompt(persona)` gains a customer-context parameter; the flow
   fetches the contact through the existing `chatwoot.get_contact()` and passes
   its attributes in. Fail-open by construction: no contact, no match, or no
   attributes yields today's behavior byte-identical. Unit-testable with
   `respx`, no live services.

3. **Contact custom attribute definitions** — Chatwoot admin UI, no code.

4. **Persona text** — the Bahana relationship-assistant framing goes in the
   operator-editable persona field. No code, and it demonstrates in passing
   that operators can tune the AI themselves.

### 5.3 Explicitly out of Phase 0

Customer 360 portfolio block; RM suggestion queue; segment/campaign builder;
BigQuery; authentication tiers; consent ledger; any real data feed.

**Authentication is deliberately absent** and this is disclosed, not hidden.
The demo shows figures to an unverified WhatsApp sender. §7.1 states what
production must do instead, and the demo narration should say so out loud —
a demo that quietly pretends the problem doesn't exist is worse than one that
names it and shows the plan.

### 5.4 Tenant

**Create a dedicated `bahana` tenant.** Do not demo on `default`.

- `add-tenant.sh bahana` — the name passes the `^[a-z][a-z0-9]*$` check, and
  hostnames are `bahana.crm.<ip>.nip.io`, so there is no DNS wait and Caddy
  obtains TLS automatically, exactly as for proton and aeon360.
- Isolation: own Chatwoot account, own Postgres, own agent. Synthetic nasabah
  stay contained and the seeder's batch purge is a clean undo.
- The URL and account name are on screen for the whole meeting. `bahana.crm…`
  is free credibility; `default` is not.
- It rehearses the provisioning story we are selling.

Diff `add-tenant.sh` and `example.env` against what aeon360 actually needed
before running it — that provisioning surfaced gaps.

### 5.5 Channel

`+16292843510` is a registered WhatsApp sender on WABA `1095367113150862`,
status **Online**, 80 MPS. No sandbox required.

- Configure the **Twilio WhatsApp inbox inside Chatwoot** (Chatwoot holds its
  own account SID / auth token / `whatsapp:+16292843510`), then point the
  number's inbound webhook at that inbox's callback URL.
- Business display name currently reads **"Demo Main Account"** — visible to
  the nasabah in WhatsApp. Changing it requires Meta review; start it early,
  and if it doesn't clear, narrate it as a demo sender.
- Twilio balance was **$6.90**. Ample for a demo, but a zero balance mid-meeting
  stops everything. Top it up.
- aeon360's number is not in this sender list, so it lives in another account or
  subaccount. Confirm before touching webhook configuration — do not repoint a
  live customer's number.

### 5.6 Demo hygiene

The platform feature switchboard is still design-only, so a new tenant does
**not** open empty: most surfaces are RBAC-permission-gated and the
`administrator` role holds every permission. Bahana's admin will therefore see
Cases, Taxonomy, RSA incidents, the DMS card and the report pages — several
automotive-flavored, and reports blank without a warehouse.

For Monday: trim `PROTON_FEATURES` in `bahana.env` and don't navigate to the
rest. **Do not build a custom role under time pressure** — a Chatwoot custom
role replaces `administrator` outright, which is how you lock yourself out of
your own demo tenant an hour before the meeting.

### 5.7 Provisioning order, long-lead first

1. Start the WhatsApp display-name change (external review queue).
2. Top up Twilio balance.
3. Diff `add-tenant.sh` / `example.env` against aeon360's requirements.
4. `add-tenant.sh bahana` on the VM.
5. Chatwoot admin user; Twilio WhatsApp inbox; point the number's webhook at it.
6. Register the agent bot and assign it to that inbox.
7. Define contact custom attributes.
8. Seed the sekuritas demo data.
9. Set the persona text.
10. Deploy `agent` with the customer-context change.
11. Rehearse end to end from a real handset.

### 5.8 Demo script

1. Show the CRM: a contact list of nasabah, sidebar showing portfolio
   attributes. State that the data is synthetic.
2. From a handset, message `+16292843510` with something mundane — a fee
   question.
3. The AI answers the question, and because it can see the profile, frames the
   answer to this nasabah and introduces the staged offer in context.
4. Interrupt from the CRM: an agent takes over mid-conversation, the bot goes
   silent, the human continues with the full profile visible beside them.
5. Show `ai_actions` — every AI decision logged with its inputs.
6. Close on the production picture: §7, and what a real data feed unlocks.

### 5.9 Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Tenant provisioning surfaces gaps like aeon360's | Do it first, not last; step 3 diffs before running |
| Contact lookup misses (phone mismatch) | Fail-open to generic behavior; rehearse from the exact handset used in the demo |
| Display name still reads "Demo Main Account" | Cosmetic; narrate it |
| Twilio balance exhausted | Topped up in step 2 |
| Anything WhatsApp-side fails on the day | Fall back to Chatwoot's website widget — identical personalization story, less impressive channel |

## 6. Production roadmap

**Phase 1 — real data.** Back-office feed into BigQuery; serving-tier
`nasabah_profile` in the backend's per-tenant Postgres; sync of the curated
subset into Chatwoot attributes. Identity resolution (§7.2). This phase is
where personalization stops being a demo.

**Phase 2 — the RM surface.** Offer catalog with compliance-owned eligibility
rules; scoring; the staged-offer batch; a suggestion queue as a Chatwoot fork
patch — today's ranked list of nasabah with the reason and an AI-drafted
message, approve-to-send. This is the direct answer to their "suggestion" ask.

**Phase 3 — governance and scale.** Authentication tiers; consent ledger and
opt-out; suppression and frequency caps; outcome attribution into the metrics
layer; segment/campaign builder for operators.

## 7. Security and compliance

Everything here is **out of Phase 0 and required for production.** It is
written for the proposal: it is the section that shows we understand what
selling to a securities firm entails.

### 7.1 Authentication before disclosure

The moment the AI states "portofolio Anda Rp X", financial data has been
disclosed to whoever is holding that handset. Phone possession is weak
authentication: SIM swap, shared and family devices, recycled numbers.

Production must run **two personalization tiers**:

- **Unverified** — product education, generic offers, no balances, no holdings,
  no transaction history.
- **Verified** — specific figures, after OTP or a deep link from their already
  authenticated app.

Designing the tier boundary in from the start costs little. Retrofitting it
after a disclosure incident costs a great deal.

### 7.2 Identity resolution

WhatsApp yields a phone number; their records are keyed by SID, client ID or
RDN. Stale numbers, family handsets and second SIMs all produce misses — and a
miss means no personalization at all. Required: an explicit unmatched path where
the AI asks to confirm identity and otherwise degrades to generic. It must never
guess a match.

### 7.3 Consent and opt-out

A consent flag on the profile, checked before any offer is staged or spoken,
and a working opt-out (`STOP` / `BERHENTI`) honored in the CRM and respected by
the batch.

### 7.4 Suitability is an eligibility rule, not a prompt instruction

"Don't offer high-risk products to a konservatif profile" written as a prompt
instruction will eventually leak. It must be a filter applied to the offer
catalog **before** the LLM sees the candidate set. Per §4.3, the LLM phrases
offers; it never selects them.

### 7.5 Audit

`ai_actions` already logs every AI decision before execution. For a regulated
buyer, "every suggestion is reproducible with the inputs that produced it" is a
selling point rather than plumbing. Retention and export belong in Phase 3.

### 7.6 Data residency

Hosting real nasabah portfolio data on our VM is a bank-grade security
conversation for a state-owned securities firm, and it can change the
deployment model entirely — their GCP project rather than ours. Synthetic data
makes this a non-issue for Phase 0. Raise it early so it does not ambush the
production negotiation. §4.1 notes the architectural fallback if they require
in-house hosting.

## 8. Positioning against Mekari Qontak

Qontak is an established SaaS omnichannel CRM and a WhatsApp BSP. Attacking it
head-on is a poor use of this opportunity.

- **Do not** pitch CRM feature parity.
- **Do** pitch the personalization layer they don't have: portfolio-aware
  conversations, next-best-action, RM suggestions, auditable AI decisions.
- Qontak can remain in place for service conversations during Phase 0 and
  Phase 1.
- If Bahana already pays Qontak for WABA hosting, riding that number is worth
  evaluating versus running our own — subject to the constraint that a WhatsApp
  number has exactly one inbound webhook, so whoever holds it owns the
  conversation. This is the same constraint that shaped the AEON360
  integration.
- Longer term, if the personalization layer proves out, absorbing the
  conversation surface is a natural expansion rather than a displacement fight.

**Cost note for the proposal:** we currently reach WhatsApp through Twilio,
which adds a per-message fee on top of Meta's. Chatwoot supports a native Meta
Cloud API channel; moving removes the Twilio markup and, as a side effect,
unlocks the WhatsApp calling path that Package C had to write off. Treat this
as a cost-optimization line item, not a launch dependency.

## 9. Open questions

1. **Will Bahana provide a back-office data feed?** This is the question that
   decides whether the product is next-best-action or better-worded blasting.
   Everything in Phase 1 depends on the answer.
2. **Where must nasabah data live?** Our infrastructure or theirs. Changes the
   deployment model (§7.6, §4.1).
3. **Who owns the offer catalog** on their side, and who signs off on
   suitability rules? Without a named owner, §4.3's separation is theoretical.
4. **Do they keep Qontak?** Affects whether we run our own WABA number or ride
   theirs (§8).
5. **Which entry points can they instrument** to drive inbound WhatsApp
   traffic (§2.3)? Without these, the no-template design has no audience in
   production.
